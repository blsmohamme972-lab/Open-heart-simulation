## Details

### 01-1
- I opened the report's own source, PMC13555479 (Ann Thorac Surg Short Reports 2026). It says: "In 2023, off-pump surgery was performed in 11.5% of isolated CABG cases (18,656 of 161,907)" and "reaching 17.9% of isolated CABG cases in 2023". Both are cited to Iribarne et al., Ann Thorac Surg 2025;119:1139-1150.
- I could not open the primary paper. The publisher returned HTTP 403, and the PubMed abstract (PMID 40127833) gives no volumes.
- Independent evidence (snippet only): the "Status of the Database" table in the STS 2026 update lists 2023 as 162,288 isolated CABG and 18,635 off-pump (= 11.48%). The same snippet says isolated CABG is 53% of STS ACSD procedures. These agree in size with the claim, but the exact count differs because the database was pulled later.
- Verdict: UNVERIFIED (snippet plus the report's own source only). For the game, "~162,000 per year, ~11.5% off-pump" is safe to use.

### 01-2
- PMC12617330 (Ogami et al., Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2025;106:3223) says: "A total of 178,014 patients undergoing isolated SAVR were identified", July 2014 to June 2022. Unmatched medians: CPB 96 min (women) and 90 min (men); cross-clamp 72 min (women) and 68 min (men). "Operative mortality was 1.9%."
- Cleveland Clinic Consult QD (22 Jan 2025), citing the STS National Database, gives a nationwide total of "18,792 in 2023", "down more than 37% from a peak of 30,171 in 2015", against more than 100,000 TAVR procedures that year.
- So "~22,000 per year" is an 8-year average and hides the decline. The current real-world figure is about 19,000 per year. Corrected in the report.

### 01-3
- STS/SCA/AmSECT 2018 (PMC5850589), read directly:
  - "It is reasonable to maintain activated clotting time above 480 seconds during CPB" (IIa C).
  - With maximal-activation or microcuvette ACT devices, "values above 400 seconds are frequently considered therapeutic".
  - Bolus example: "a fixed, weight-based dose (e.g. 300 IU/kg)".
  - "six of the 100 patients that received 300 IU/kg ... a resultant post-heparin ACT < 350 seconds".
- Independent sources:
  - ISTH SSC 2024: "480 seconds or higher" is the most often reported target. It proposes defining heparin resistance as failing to reach ACT ≥ 480 s after 500 U/kg.
  - EACTS 2019: "most units aim for an ACT of >480 s"; starting dose "300 to 500 U/kg".
  - Medicina 2025 review: "Initial bolus of 300–400 IU/kg"; heparin resistance prevalence "4% to 30.7%".
- Game note: 6% is one study's result. A random-event probability anywhere from 4% to 30% could be justified, depending on how resistance is defined.

### 01-4
- 2024 EACTS/EACTAIC/EBCP, Recommendation Table 38 (read from the table image):
  - "A protamine-to-heparin ratio < 1.0 is recommended to avoid overdosing of protamine": Class I, Level B, refs 489 and 492.
  - "Cardiotomy suction should be discontinued with the start of the administration of protamine": IIa C.
  - The narrative text adds: "The optimal protamine-to-heparin ratio should be below 1.0."
- STS 2018: "It is reasonable to limit the ratio of protamine/heparin to less than 2.6 mg protamine/100 Units of heparin" (IIa C).
- The two rules do not conflict. The 2024 limit is simply stricter.

### 01-5
- The 2024 text (section 9.5) says: "most perfusionists usually set the pump flow rate between 2.2 and 2.8 L/min/m2". The 2019 text says the same.
- Section 5.1: "the systolic arterial blood pressure (ABP) is commonly kept below 100 mmHg to lower the risk of aortic dissection". The 2019 text adds that dissection happens in 0.06-0.23% of aortic cannulations.
- Recommendation Table 45 (image), 2024:
  - "It is recommended that PRBCs be transfused during CPB if the HCT value is <18% (Hb 6.0 g/dL)": I C.
  - "For HCT values between 18% and 24%, PRBCs may be considered based on an assessment of the adequacy of tissue oxygenation": IIb B. The footnote reads: "The DO2 is maintained at >273 mL/min/m2 and cerebral oximetry is satisfied."
  - "PRBCs are not recommended to be transfused during CPB if the HCT is >24% and DO2 and extraction are acceptable": III C.
- The report's "HCT 21-24% acceptable if DO2 > 273" comes from the narrative sentence "Existing guidelines suggest ...". Corrected to the formal table.

### 01-6
- JECT 2014 (Kim et al.):
  - del Nido: "administer 20 mL/kg with a maximum dose of 1000 mL for patients larger than 50 kg"; half dose if the clamp is expected to last < 30 min; "After 90 minutes of aortic clamp time, the surgeon decides how much more ...".
  - Modified Buckberg: 4:1, induction and maintenance at 4 °C, maintenance "every 15–20 minutes", reperfusion "hot shot" at 37 °C (~500 mL in Table 1).
- EACTS 2024: "It can be administered as a single dose for up to 90 min of ischaemia".
- Goh et al., JECT 2020: "A single dose (20 mL/kg) of DNC provides adequate myocardial protection for ∼90 minutes", possibly up to 120 min.
- Practice varies. One adult protocol uses 1000-1200 mL, then 500 mL every 60 min (snippet).

### 01-7
- The full 2024 text (section 7.5) says: antegrade "at a pressure of 60–100 mmHg"; retrograde "using a flow rate of 200–400 mL/min to a coronary sinus pressure between 30 and 50 mmHg ... Higher pressures should be avoided"; "Usually, cardioplegia is administered cold and intermittently every 20 to 30 min".
- The 2019 guideline (PMC10634377) uses the same wording.
- Older reviews (snippet) usually cap coronary sinus pressure at 40 mmHg and set warm retrograde flow at ≥ 200 mL/min.
- Game: a 40-50 mmHg warning band, with injury risk above 50, fits all of these sources.

### 01-8
- Takami 2024 (J Cardiothorac Surg 19:635):
  - "Qm > 15 ml/min, PI < 5.0, DF > 50%, SRF < 4% ... suggesting patent graft".
  - Stricter consensus: "Qm > 20 mL/min, PI < 3.0, and SFR < 3%".
  - "it is difficult to determine the definitive cut-off values".
- Independent: Brereton 2018 (Ann Cardiothorac Surg 7:710):
  - "A pulsatility index of greater than 5 should be regarded with great concern."
  - "less than 3 is desirable" for left-ventricle grafts.
  - "A measured flow of less than 15 mL per minute correlates with poorer graft patency."
  - "Diastolic flow should be greater than 50%."
- Tokuda 2007 ROC (snippet): ≤ 15 mL/min, PI ≥ 5.1, backflow ≥ 4.1% for left targets; 20 mL/min, 4.7, 4.6% for right targets.

### 01-9
- PMC7525868 (Indian J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2019/2020): "the optimal initial energy for open-chest defibrillation is 10 to 30 J". In that paper, shocks were given after de-airing, with core temperature > 30 °C.
- CALS-ANZ 2022: 20 J internal, "up to three stacked shocks", chest reopened "within 5 minutes", DOO pacing.
- BJA Education 2017 and LITFL (snippets): 20 J internal, and 5 J if the patient is on bypass.
- AATS TSRA primer (snippet): "most commonly with 10 to 20 joules".
- A 20 J game default is well supported.

### 01-10
- PMC6950894 (J Cardiothorac Surg 2020), Table 2:
  - 51 patients, elective isolated on-pump CABG.
  - Grafts median 3 (IQR 2.5-4); skin-to-skin 240 (195-275); CPB 112 (82.5-128.0); clamp 65 (48.5-80.0) min.
  - In this feasibility study, every distal anastomosis was also checked with epicardial ultrasound. My inference is that this may have lengthened times slightly.
- REPLICCAR II (PMC10903005; 3,090 patients, 5 centres): CPB 76.78 ± 27.53 min, clamp 58.22 ± 23.36 min. In-hospital mortality OR was 4.22 and 7.33 for the two CPB > 140 min groups.

### 01-11

  - "Routine intraoperative graft flow measurement should be considered" IIa B.
  - "Skeletonized IMA dissection is recommended in patients with a high risk of sternal wound infection" I B.

### 01-12

## Resolved open questions

1. **Did the 2024 ESC chronic coronary syndrome guideline replace the 2018 CABG technique classes?** Resolved: no.
   - I extracted the full PDF of the 2024 ESC CCS guideline (Vrints et al., Eur Heart J 2024, doi 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae177) and searched it. It has no recommendations on skeletonization, endoscopic or no-touch vein harvest, or graft flow measurement. It only states that LIMA-to-LAD and multiple arterial grafting reduce graft occlusion.
   - The 2018 ESC/EACTS table (see 01-11) is still the latest graded ESC/EACTS source.
   - An October 2024 ESC Working Group / EACTS Coronary Task Force clinical consensus statement (Sandner et al., Eur Heart J 2024) gives ungraded advice that matches it: skeletonized ITA when sternal-wound risk is high (especially bilateral ITA); endoscopic vein harvest when leg-wound risk is high, "considering harvester experience"; open, preferably no-touch, vein harvest when leg risk is low. Source: [ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/ten-points-to-remember/2024/10/21/15/38/intraoperative-and-postoperative). Grade A for the CCS check, B for the consensus summary.
2. **2019 EACTS MAP target 50-80 mmHg Class I A (seen only through an editorial snippet).** Resolved by reading the recommendation table images.
   - 2019: "It is recommended to adjust the MAP during CPB with the use of arterial vasodilators (if MAP >80 mmHg) or vasoconstrictors (if MAP <50 mmHg) ..." is Class I, Level A. The text adds "the MAP during CPB is usually accepted in a range of 50–80 mmHg".
   - 2024 (Table 40): "It is recommended that the MAP be maintained between 50 and 80 mmHg ..." is Class I, Level A. Vasopressors to push MAP above 80 mmHg are not recommended (III B). Targeting individual cerebral autoregulation limits should be considered (IIa A).
   - Grade A. [2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10634377); [2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11826094/).
3. **Current annual count for isolated SAVR.** Resolved for 2023: 18,792 isolated SAVR (STS National Database), down > 37% from a peak of 30,171 in 2015. That same year saw more than 100,000 TAVR procedures (100,501 commercial TAVR at 838 US sites). Grade B ([Cleveland Clinic Consult QD, Jan 2025](https://consultqd.clevelandclinic.org/how-and-why-volumes-matter-in-aortic-valve-replacement)). A figure of 17,568 for 2022 appeared only in a search summary, and I could not find its source. Isolated mitral repair counts for 2023-2025 are still not found.
4. **Cannula French sizes.** Partly resolved, at grade C (catalogue and device-database snippets only):
   - Medtronic MC2 two-stage venous cannula: GUDID/FDA device entries list 34-46 Fr and 36-46 Fr.
   - Other catalogues list two-stage sizes 32/40, 36/46 and 36/51 Fr, and adult aortic cannulas of 20, 22 and 24 Fr (Edwards EZ Glide 21 and 24 Fr).
   - The FDA 510(k) K140165 PDF is a scanned image and could not be read. Manufacturer IFU pressure-drop charts are still not verified.
