## Why the previous image is rejected

The AI storyboard was an appearance invention, not a device- or patient-specific reconstruction. Its chunky opaque distal body, conspicuous raised circular tissue rim and uniformly polished organ treatment have no verified matching reference. The sequence is static and cannot demonstrate tool–tissue synchronization. Retain it for history, not as an accepted visual target.

## Selected device reference

Use one consistent family for this experiment: **Medtronic DLP Curved Tip Arterial Cannulae with One-piece Wirewound Body**, illustrated on printed page 14 (PDF page index 13) of the [official 2024 catalogue](https://www.medtronic.com/content/dam/medtronic-wide/public/western-europe/products/cardiac-vascular/cardiovascular/cannulae/cannula-catalog-interactive-2024-en-gb.pdf).

The page identifies a curved beveled tip, tapered reinforced body, orientation line and round flange. The 20 Fr variant is listed as 6.7 mm, with 27.9 cm overall length for this family. These are device reference dimensions, not insertion instructions. The photographed body is slender and translucent, with fine visible reinforcement; the prominent opaque elbow in storyboard 001 is not a faithful representation of this selected family.

The catalogue is a **reference-only manufacturer publication**. Its PDF and page image stay outside the repository. No manufacturer CAD, exact surface reconstruction, product endorsement or permission to redistribute its imagery is claimed. Original procedural geometry may approximate the visible family; any unverified dimensions must remain identified as artist choices. Do not mix another family's tip, flange or connector into a supposed exact replica.

The article specifies CC BY 2.0. The figure is kept outside the repository for this review; link to its source rather than silently presenting it as our own asset. It was not used as a patient texture map. Tissue appearance varies with exposure, pathology, lighting and physiological state; no single reference photograph defines universal normal appearance.

## Actual motion experiment

Build a small original Blender scene of an **already seated cannula** and a local vascular surface. Show only the attachment/motion relationship; this is not an insertion tutorial or a completed full-heart simulator. Use shared geometric ownership for the tissue contact region and the cannula attachment, with slight external tube compliance. Keep the camera fixed so camera movement cannot conceal drift.

Motion is artist-authored until compared with appropriate time-resolved evidence. Do not claim that a sine wave, a smooth loop or an attachment constraint reproduces physiological forces. Do not label a stylized tissue surface a measured patient reconstruction. No actual phone benchmark is implied by an offline Blender render.

## What a credible resemblance claim would require

| Dimension | Comparison needed |
|---|---|
| Device shape | Selected product/size, calibrated photographs or cleared CAD, silhouette and dimension checks |
| Anatomical scene | Reviewed anatomy and matching exposure/state; identified landmarks and uncertain regions |
| Materials | Matched view, lighting/exposure and color calibration; wetness, translucency and tissue texture review |
| Motion | Suitable time-resolved reference, tracked trajectories, timing/phase and deformation comparisons |
| Contact | Visible and geometric attachment agreement through motion, with clipping/sliding checks |
| Runtime | The same scene tested on named Android hardware at stated quality and thermal conditions |

A global “99%” cannot be inferred from one image-similarity score, frame rate, or a subjective impression. Define the metric, reference set, uncertainty and independent review before reporting any percentage. This iteration can improve inspectable features and supply a motion sample; it cannot certify 99% realism.
